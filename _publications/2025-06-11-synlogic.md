*Equal contribution

**Abstract:** In this paper, we study logical reasoning -- a high-level cognitive ability that, while closely tied to language, relies heavily on abstract, rule-based patterns -- and ask whether existing LLMs can develop such an ability through reinforcement learning alone, without requiring human-annotated reasoning traces. We present SynLogic, a scalable framework for synthesizing verifiable logical reasoning data using LLMs. SynLogic generates reasoning steps from a strong teacher model, then applies a verification mechanism to identify and filter correct ones. This distilled, verifiable dataset is then used to train a smaller model via reinforcement learning. Experiments across five logical reasoning benchmarks demonstrate that SynLogic-trained models outperform those trained on model-generated data without verification, as well as models trained on self-generated data. Our results suggest that self-verifiable data synthesis can effectively transfer abstract reasoning capabilities from strong models to smaller ones through reinforcement learning.
