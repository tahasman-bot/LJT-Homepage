**Abstract:** We propose using in-context sharpness as a signal for detecting hallucination in large language models. Sharpness, measured by the sensitivity of model representations to token-level perturbations, correlates strongly with whether the model is generating factually accurate or hallucinated content. By monitoring sharpness at each generation step, we can identify potential hallucinations before they are finalized. Our experiments show that this approach is model-agnostic and works across multiple benchmarks. We further demonstrate that sharpness-based alerts can be used to guide decoding, reducing hallucination rates without degrading generation quality.
