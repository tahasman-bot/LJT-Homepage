**Abstract:** We identify a universal truthfulness hyperplane in the internal representations of large language models (LLMs) that consistently separates truthful from non-truthful states across layers, model families, and tasks. Using linear probing and dimensionality reduction, we show that this hyperplane emerges naturally during pretraining and becomes increasingly pronounced with model scale. The direction corresponding to truthfulness alignment is robust to paraphrasing and task variations, and we demonstrate that steering representations toward this direction during generation improves factuality. Our findings suggest that model-internal truth detection is not a task-specific phenomenon but reflects a global property of LLM representations.
